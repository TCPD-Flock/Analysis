B1

Purpose of this project

This page compiles publicly available information, records-request activity, the department's own public statements, and a full statistical analysis of the department's system access/audit logs, about the automated license plate reader (ALPR) program run by The Colony, TX Police Department (TCPD) through its vendor Flock Safety. It's intended as a neutral reference, not an argument for or against the technology — claims are attributed to their source, and unresolved points are labeled as such rather than presented as settled.

B5

The department's public position

Summarized from Chief Foxall's August 2026 Council presentation, cross-checked where possible against the audit-log analysis in the other tabs.

Legal basis

Photographing a plate on a public road is not a Fourth Amendment search, per ~36 cited court decisions including a March 2026 Fifth Circuit case (not independently confirmed). Warrant not required because the City treats resulting data as city-owned.

Scope

21 cameras cover <2% of ~450 lane-miles; fewer than 1.75% of reads are ever reviewed by a person (department estimate, not independently verified).

Data sharing

Described as limited to agencies in Texas or within 500 miles, each external request requiring individual written approval. The audit data directly contradicts the scope of this claim for the 31 months preceding it — see the Out-of-Network tab: 40.5% of network searches from Jan 2024–Jul 2026 came from beyond 500 miles, falling to 0.34% only in the partial month this statement was made.

Accountability

Acknowledged officer misuse happens nationally (mostly tracking partners/exes); stated a monthly audit is conducted by the Assistant Chief. Did not state whether TCPD itself has had confirmed misuse incidents — see the Risk tab for what the data shows on this specific point.

B6

Open questions

  • Now answered by the data: whether sharing is really limited to Texas/500 miles — see the Out-of-Network tab. It was not, at least not as a hard technical limit, for the 31 months before the claim was made: 40.5% of network searches (Jan 2024–Jul 2026) originated beyond that radius, every single month. That dropped to 0.34% starting the same month the claim was made to Council.
  • Still open: whether TCPD has had any confirmed internal misuse incidents. The Risk tab shows a data pattern consistent with the nationally-documented misuse signature, spanning 10 of the department's officers (4 of them account for most of the 28 flagged pairs) — but the audit log alone can't establish intent. That requires the department's own explanation of the flagged rows.
  • Still open: the true net cost of the ALPR program, given the 2026 agreement's pro-rata credit against prior contracts.
  • Still open: why the City's own PO system shows only one PO for Flock despite multiple signed contracts.
  • Still open: what direction, if any, Council gave staff after the July/August 2026 opposition testimony.
B7

Regional & national context

Other jurisdictions' actions on Flock/ALPR, for context — not an implication that the same concerns apply to The Colony specifically.

  • Wylie, TX (Collin County) — Council voted 7-0 (Aug 2026) to terminate its Flock contract over data-access concerns.
  • Pflugerville, TX — cancelled after a resident's PIA request found the city's data had been accessed by numerous outside agencies; the school district followed.
  • Hood County, TX — commissioners terminated their Flock agreement after resident concerns.
  • Denton, TX — active resident petition (~500 signatures) to remove the city's 10 cameras.
  • Carrollton, TX — residents have formally asked the city to end its contract; not yet done.
  • Statewide — Gov. Abbott directed state agencies to halt Flock funding (Aug 2026), citing a Lufkin PD officer misuse case.
  • Nationally — at least 56 cities/towns deactivated or cancelled Flock contracts in 2026; Florida became the first state to ban ALPR systems outright (Sept 2026).

What actually happened in January 2026 — now confirmed directly against the raw textStarting that month, the Reason field stops containing freeform text almost entirely, in both logs simultaneously, replaced by a mandatory category dropdown (48 fixed categories for TCPD, ~1,715 agency-configured categories network-wide). This is a genuine taxonomy, not a rubber stamp: concentration in the single most-used category fell after the switch in both logs (Org 16.2%→10.3%, Network 7.2%→4.3%), and 41-63% of post-cutover rows still carry a free-text suffix with real detail. It's arguably a good change — dropdowns are exactly the kind of guardrail that would have stopped East Tawakoni PD's single-letter pattern — but it does mean 2024/2025 justification-quality numbers can't be compared apples-to-apples against 2026 using the same blank/vague heuristic. Investigation 1 · Repeated-target pattern — is anyone doing this without a real case?

Only possible in the Org log, since it's the only place plates and officer names are both unredacted. Grouped every (officer, plate) pair and scored each one on: is it manual (search) vs. likely-automated (lookup); does it ever have a case number attached; how vague is the stated reason; how concentrated at odd hours; and how long a span it's spread across. That combination — manual, no case, vague, spread over weeks or months — is the signature described nationally as associated with personal/stalking misuse, as distinct from a legitimate high-volume investigation.

Direct answer: is anyone following a plate that isn't tied to a real case?

Composite scoring across all 1,785 (officer, plate) pairs with 3+ searches — independently re-derived twice from the raw CSVs since first published, most recently Sept 2026

Yes, by this definition — but not at extreme volume, and spread wider across the department than first reported. 161 (officer, plate) pairs show searches spread 14+ days apart; 128 of those never have a case number attached. Restricting to the ones that are also predominantly manual and clearly-scored (composite score ≥ 1.0 — the same bar used throughout this page) narrows that to 28 pairs across 10 officers: Officer-04 (8 of the 28), Officer-01 (6), Officer-09 (4), Officer-11 (4), and one each for Officer-06, Officer-07, Officer-08, Officer-10, Officer-20, and Officer-38. The other 100 of the 128 long-span/no-case pairs score near zero because they're almost entirely automated hotlist hits (lookup, not search) rather than an officer choosing to re-search — the scoring formula correctly discounts those; see the methodology note below. None of the 28 reach anywhere near the volume of the 964-search hotlist case below — they're 3 to 16 hits each — which is itself informative: the shape that looks most like sustained personal interest is low-and-slow, not high-volume.

Q. Is anyone searching the same plate over and over without it being tied to a real case — the pattern linked to stalking/personal misuse elsewhere?Yes, at low volume
28 (officer, plate) pairs show manual, repeated searches spread 14 days to 15 months apart with no case number ever recorded, spanning 10 officers — 4 of them (Officer-04, Officer-01, Officer-09, Officer-11) account for 22 of the 28, the other 6 officers appear once each. None reach high volume — 3 to 16 hits each — which is itself the point: the highest-volume repeat pattern (964 hits) looks automated and case-linked, while the pattern that looks most like sustained personal interest is quiet and low-volume. See Repeated-Target & Stalking Risk.
Q. Are people accessing the system at odd hours, and does that look different from daytime use?Yes, measurably
Late-night (1–5am) search share roughly doubled from 2024 to 2025 (4.5% → 10.2%) and held into 2026. More importantly: once you isolate genuinely blank/vague reasons from the separate case-number-habit, late-night searches are 3.4× more likely to carry no real justification than daytime searches, and weekend searches 2.2× more likely than weekday. That's a real documented correlation, not just "police work nights." See Off-Hours.
Q. How does 2024 vs. 2025 vs. 2026 compare?Growth, then plateau
Network search volume: +128% 2024→2025, then +9% 2025→2026 (Jan–Aug each year). Org log: +83% then −5%. Out-of-radius share of network traffic fell (50.5% → 40.9% → 31.8%) even as absolute out-of-radius volume grew. Federal share of traffic rose roughly 5× (0.17% → 0.54% → 0.84%). Distinct partner agencies per month cratered 74% starting Jan 2026. Full detail in Timeline & Trends.
Q. Has justification quality gotten better or worse over time?Can't fully say — the measuring stick changed
Resolved by direct investigation: the Reason field switched to mandatory category dropdowns in January 2026 in both logs — confirmed as a real 48-category (TCPD) / ~1,715-category (network-wide, agency-configured) taxonomy, not a rubber stamp; single-value concentration fell after the switch rather than spiking. Comparing only the two years where the field was still freeform text: it improved (Org log 39.2% → 26.2% vague/case-number-only, 2024 vs. 2025, Jan–Aug). See each investigation's Justification tab for the full breakdown.
Q. Is the department's network exposure to outside agencies growing or shrinking?Both, depending what you measure
Absolute out-of-radius search volume grew every year. Out-of-radius share of total traffic shrank, because in-state volume grew faster. The number of distinct partner agencies with any access collapsed 74% starting Jan 2026 while total volume kept climbing — meaning fewer, bigger partners rather than a genuinely smaller footprint. All three of these are real and worth presenting together, since any one alone tells a misleadingly simple story.
Q. Is ICE or CBP involved?No active tag found
No entry across 17.1M network rows is tagged ICE or CBP. US Border Patrol and Homeland Security Investigations both appear but are explicitly marked inactive in the export, meaning access was later revoked. Federal access overall (mostly FBI and USPIS) grew substantially as a share of traffic, and July 2026 saw an unexplained 18,417-search FBI spike worth asking about directly.
Q. Can this data tell manual, curiosity-driven searches apart from automated hotlist hits?Partially
The Search Type field distinguishes search (manual query) from lookup (more often tied to a standing hotlist alert firing automatically on every camera hit). That distinction is what separates the 964-hit case — mostly lookup, almost certainly automated — from the 28-pair "sustained, no case" list, which is mostly manual search type. It can't tell you what was in an officer's head, only which button they pressed.
Q. Is this bidirectional — do Colony's own officers reach into far-away out-of-state networks too, not just get searched by others?Yes
The Org log's Filters field shows Colony officers voluntarily narrowing or expanding searches to specific states — Texas most often (7,861 searches), but also Arkansas (138), Georgia (96), Florida (58), Oklahoma (55), and Illinois (55). The access relationship runs both directions; this report focuses on inbound access because that's where the "Texas / 500 miles" public claim was made, but it's worth asking whether Colony officers' own outbound reach is governed by the same policy.
Q. Is one officer or agency responsible for a disproportionate and growing share of activity?Yes, and concentrated
Top 10 of 104 officers account for 53% of all Colony searches; top 10 of 5,363 agencies account for 29% of all network searches, with Houston TX PD alone at nearly 1 in 10. See Volume.

Methodology & limitations

  • Vague/blank classification is a text-pattern heuristic (regex + fixed generic-term list), not a manual read of every row — see the Fort Worth PD caveat: its 15.7% flag rate is mostly terse-but-legible shorthand ("inv", "sus", radio codes), a lower-confidence flag than the Tawakoni single-character pattern.
  • The repeated-target risk score (Risk tab) is a transparent composite — manual-search fraction × (reduced weight if any case number is ever attached) × reason-vagueness × a duration factor × an off-hours concentration factor — shown with its component parts in the table rather than as a black-box number, specifically so it can be second-guessed.
  • Resolved: Starting January 2026, Flock's Reason field switched from freeform text to mandatory category dropdowns in both logs simultaneously. Directly investigated against the raw reason strings: it's a genuine closed taxonomy (48 categories for TCPD, ~1,715 agency-configured network-wide), and single-value concentration fell rather than spiked after the switch — not evidence of rubber-stamping. It still means 2026 reason-quality figures aren't directly comparable to 2024–2025 using the same blank/vague heuristic; category-level analysis is used instead for 2026 onward.
  • The August 2026 data point in every timeline chart is a partial month (through Aug 13 only, per the records request cutoff) and is shown dashed/lighter — don't read its lower totals as a real drop.
  • Off-hours analysis has no duty-roster data to compare against; elevated late-night/weekend shares should not be read as evidence of misconduct on their own. The reason-quality-by-time-of-day cross-tab is a stronger, decomposed signal than raw timing alone — see Off-Hours tab.
  • Out-of-state distance is computed from state population centroids with manual spot-corrections for large border-state agencies (e.g., Memphis/Shelby County TN reclassified from "outside" to "inside" the 500-mile line after checking actual city coordinates). It's a screening tool, not a survey-grade distance — agencies very near a state line may be misclassified individually even though the aggregate picture is robust.
  • All figures are reproducible from the source CSVs using the file + row-ID citations throughout this report.
  • Raw plate numbers are intentionally omitted from this report; flagged entries are cited by source file and row ID so they can be pulled back up from the original export without re-publishing PII here.
